Model Assessment and Comparisons (WAIC)

Sudipto Banerjee

Model selection and assessment are central to Bayesian data analysis. The Watanabe-Akaike Information Criterion (WAIC)—also known as the Widely Applicable Information Criterion—is a fully Bayesian metric for estimating out-of-sample predictive accuracy. Unlike the Akaike Information Criterion (AIC) or Deviance Information Criterion (DIC), WAIC utilizes the full posterior distribution (rather than point estimates) and is valid for complex, non-linear, or singular models.

WAIC is defined on the deviance scale as:

\[\text{WAIC} = -2 \left( lppd - p_{\text{WAIC}} \right)\]

where: * \(lppd\) is the log point predictive density, measuring model fit. * \(p_{\text{WAIC}}\) is the effective number of parameters, penalizing model complexity.

1. Exact Closed-Form Derivation of \(lppd\)

The log point predictive density is defined analytically as:

\[lppd_{\text{exact}} = \sum_{i=1}^n \log p(y_i \mid y) = \sum_{i=1}^n \log \left( \iint p(y_i \mid \beta, \sigma^2) \, p(\beta, \sigma^2 \mid y) \, d\beta \, d\sigma^2 \right)\]

Posterior Parameters of the Conjugate Model

Under the conjugate prior setup:

\[\begin{aligned} \sigma^2 &\sim \text{Inverse-Gamma}(a_0, b_0) \\ \beta \mid \sigma^2 &\sim \mathcal{N}_p\left(m_0, \, \sigma^2 M_0\right) \end{aligned}\]

The joint posterior distribution is Normal-Inverse-Gamma, \(p(\beta, \sigma^2 \mid y) = \mathcal{N}_p(\mu_\beta, \sigma^2 M) \times \text{Inverse-Gamma}(a^*, b^*)\), where:

\[\begin{aligned} M &= \left(M_0^{-1} + X^\top X\right)^{-1} \\ \mu_\beta &= M \left(M_0^{-1} m_0 + X^\top y\right) \quad \text{(Posterior mean of } \beta\text{)} \\ a^* &= a_0 + \frac{n}{2} \\ b^* &= b_0 + \frac{1}{2} \left[ y^\top y + m_0^\top M_0^{-1} m_0 - \mu_\beta^\top M^{-1} \mu_\beta \right] \end{aligned}\]

Step 1: Conditional Density Given \(\sigma^2\)

For an in-sample observation \(y_i = x_i^\top \beta + \epsilon_i\) (where \(x_i^\top\) is the \(i\)-th row of \(X\)), conditioning on \(\sigma^2\) and integrating over \(\beta\) yields:

\[\mathbb{E}[y_i \mid \sigma^2, y] = x_i^\top \mathbb{E}[\beta \mid \sigma^2, y] = x_i^\top \mu_\beta\]

\[\text{Var}(y_i \mid \sigma^2, y) = x_i^\top \text{Var}(\beta \mid \sigma^2, y) x_i + \sigma^2 = \sigma^2 \left(1 + x_i^\top M x_i\right)\]

Letting \(h_i = 1 + x_i^\top M x_i\) (a leverage factor reflecting posterior covariance on observation \(i\)), the conditional distribution is:

\[y_i \mid \sigma^2, y \sim \mathcal{N}\left(x_i^\top \mu_\beta, \, \sigma^2 h_i\right)\]

Step 2: Marginalizing Out Variance \(\sigma^2\)

Marginalizing \(y_i\) over the Inverse-Gamma posterior \(p(\sigma^2 \mid y) = \frac{(b^*)^{a^*}}{\Gamma(a^*)} (\sigma^2)^{-(a^* + 1)} \exp\left(-\frac{b^*}{\sigma^2}\right)\):

\[p(y_i \mid y) = \int_0^\infty \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi \sigma^2 h_i}} \exp\left( -\frac{(y_i - x_i^\top \mu_\beta)^2}{2 \sigma^2 h_i} \right) \frac{(b^*)^{a^*}}{\Gamma(a^*)} (\sigma^2)^{-(a^* + 1)} \exp\left(-\frac{b^*}{\sigma^2}\right) d\sigma^2\]

Combining powers of \(\sigma^2\) and exponential terms inside the integral:

\[p(y_i \mid y) = \frac{(b^*)^{a^*}}{\Gamma(a^*) \sqrt{2\pi h_i}} \int_0^\infty (\sigma^2)^{-\left(a^* + \frac{1}{2} + 1\right)} \exp\left( -\frac{1}{\sigma^2} \left[ b^* + \frac{(y_i - x_i^\top \mu_\beta)^2}{2 h_i} \right] \right) d\sigma^2\]

Recognizing the kernel of an Inverse-Gamma distribution with shape \(a^* + \frac{1}{2}\) and scale \(b^* + \frac{(y_i - x_i^\top \mu_\beta)^2}{2 h_i}\), the integral evaluates to:

\[p(y_i \mid y) = \frac{(b^*)^{a^*}}{\Gamma(a^*) \sqrt{2\pi h_i}} \cdot \frac{\Gamma\left(a^* + \frac{1}{2}\right)}{\left[ b^* + \frac{(y_i - x_i^\top \mu_\beta)^2}{2 h_i} \right]^{a^* + \frac{1}{2}}}\]

Rearranging terms:

\[p(y_i \mid y) = \frac{\Gamma\left(a^* + \frac{1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma(a^*) \sqrt{2\pi b^* h_i}} \left( 1 + \frac{(y_i - x_i^\top \mu_\beta)^2}{2 b^* h_i} \right)^{-\left(a^* + \frac{1}{2}\right)}\]

This corresponds exactly to a non-standardized univariate Student’s \(t\)-distribution \(t_{\nu}(\mu_i, s_i^2)\) with: * Degrees of freedom: \(\nu = 2a^*\) * Location parameter: \(\mu_i = x_i^\top \mu_\beta\) * Scale parameter squared: \(s_i^2 = \frac{b^*}{a^*} h_i = \frac{b^*}{a^*}\left(1 + x_i^\top M x_i\right)\)

Step 3: Exact Closed-Form \(lppd\) Formula

Taking the natural logarithm and summing over all \(n\) observations gives the exact analytical formula:

\[lppd_{\text{exact}} = \sum_{i=1}^n \left[ \log \Gamma\left(a^* + \frac{1}{2}\right) - \log \Gamma(a^*) - \frac{1}{2} \log\left(2\pi b^* h_i\right) - \left(a^* + \frac{1}{2}\right) \log\left( 1 + \frac{(y_i - x_i^\top \mu_\beta)^2}{2 b^* h_i} \right) \right]\]

2. Exact Closed-Form Derivation of \(p_{\text{WAIC}}\)

While \(p_{\text{WAIC}}\) is typically evaluated via sample statistics, for conjugate Bayesian linear regression it can also be calculated in exact closed form using the Law of Total Variance over the posterior parameters \((\beta, \sigma^2)\).

We evaluate the posterior variance of the log-likelihood for observation \(i\):

\[\text{Var}_{\beta, \sigma^2 \mid y}(LL_i) = \text{Var}_{\beta, \sigma^2 \mid y} \left( -\frac{1}{2}\log(2\pi) - \frac{1}{2}\log\sigma^2 - \frac{(y_i - x_i^\top \beta)^2}{2\sigma^2} \right)\]

Let \(e_i = y_i - x_i^\top \mu_\beta\) denote the posterior mean residual and \(k_i = x_i^\top M x_i\) denote the leverage of observation \(i\).

Step 1: Conditioning on \(\sigma^2\) (Inner Moments)

For a fixed \(\sigma^2\), the conditional distribution is \(y_i - x_i^\top \beta \sim \mathcal{N}(e_i, \sigma^2 k_i)\):

  1. Conditional Expectation: \[\mathbb{E}_{\beta \mid \sigma^2, y}[LL_i] = -\frac{1}{2}\log(2\pi) - \frac{1}{2}\log\sigma^2 - \frac{e_i^2 + \sigma^2 k_i}{2\sigma^2} = -\frac{1}{2}\log(2\pi) - \frac{1}{2}\log\sigma^2 - \frac{e_i^2}{2\sigma^2} - \frac{k_i}{2}\]

  2. Conditional Variance: Using the variance property of non-central chi-squared quadratic forms \(\text{Var}\left((y_i - x_i^\top \beta)^2\right) = 2(\sigma^2 k_i)^2 + 4(\sigma^2 k_i)e_i^2\): \[\text{Var}_{\beta \mid \sigma^2, y}(LL_i) = \frac{1}{4\sigma^4} \left[ 2\sigma^4 k_i^2 + 4\sigma^2 k_i e_i^2 \right] = \frac{k_i^2}{2} + \frac{k_i e_i^2}{\sigma^2}\]

Step 2: Applying the Law of Total Variance over \(\sigma^2 \mid y\)

\[\text{Var}_{\beta, \sigma^2 \mid y}(LL_i) = \underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{\sigma^2 \mid y}\left[ \text{Var}_{\beta \mid \sigma^2, y}(LL_i) \right]}_{\text{Term 1}} + \underbrace{\text{Var}_{\sigma^2 \mid y}\left( \mathbb{E}_{\beta \mid \sigma^2, y}[LL_i] \right)}_{\text{Term 2}}\]

Evaluating Term 1:

Using \(\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{\sigma^2}\right] = \frac{a^*}{b^*}\) under \(\sigma^2 \mid y \sim \text{Inverse-Gamma}(a^*, b^*)\): \[\text{Term 1} = \mathbb{E}_{\sigma^2 \mid y}\left[ \frac{k_i^2}{2} + \frac{k_i e_i^2}{\sigma^2} \right] = \frac{k_i^2}{2} + \frac{a^* k_i e_i^2}{b^*}\]

Evaluating Term 2:

Expanding the variance of the conditional expectation: \[\text{Term 2} = \text{Var}_{\sigma^2 \mid y}\left( -\frac{1}{2}\log\sigma^2 - \frac{e_i^2}{2\sigma^2} \right) = \frac{1}{4}\text{Var}(\log\sigma^2) + \frac{e_i^4}{4}\text{Var}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma^2}\right) + \frac{e_i^2}{2}\text{Cov}\left(\log\sigma^2, \frac{1}{\sigma^2}\right)\]

Under precision \(\tau = \frac{1}{\sigma^2} \sim \text{Gamma}(a^*, b^*)\): * \(\text{Var}(\log\sigma^2) = \text{Var}(\log\tau) = \psi^{(1)}(a^*)\)    (where \(\psi^{(1)}\) is the trigamma function) * \(\text{Var}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma^2}\right) = \text{Var}(\tau) = \frac{a^*}{(b^*)^2}\) * \(\text{Cov}\left(\log\sigma^2, \frac{1}{\sigma^2}\right) = \text{Cov}(-\log\tau, \tau) = -\frac{1}{b^*}\)

Substituting these exact identities into Term 2: \[\text{Term 2} = \frac{1}{4} \psi^{(1)}(a^*) + \frac{a^* e_i^4}{4 (b^*)^2} - \frac{e_i^2}{2 b^*}\]

Step 3: Exact Closed-Form \(p_{\text{WAIC}}\) Formula

Combining Term 1 and Term 2 yields the exact pointwise log-likelihood variance:

\[\text{Var}(LL_i) = \frac{k_i^2}{2} + \frac{a^* k_i e_i^2}{b^*} + \frac{1}{4} \psi^{(1)}(a^*) + \frac{a^* e_i^4}{4 (b^*)^2} - \frac{e_i^2}{2 b^*}\]

Summing over all \(n\) observations provides the exact analytical effective number of parameters:

\[p_{\text{WAIC}, \text{exact}} = \sum_{i=1}^n \left[ \frac{k_i^2}{2} + \frac{a^* k_i e_i^2}{b^*} + \frac{1}{4} \psi^{(1)}(a^*) + \frac{a^* e_i^4}{4 (b^*)^2} - \frac{e_i^2}{2 b^*} \right]\]

Thus, the exact closed-form WAIC is given by:

\[\text{WAIC}_{\text{exact}} = -2 \left( lppd_{\text{exact}} - p_{\text{WAIC}, \text{exact}} \right)\]

3. Sample-Based Computation of WAIC

When using posterior draws \(\theta^{(s)} = \left(\beta^{(s)}, \sigma^{2(s)}\right)\) for \(s = 1, \ldots, N\), WAIC is evaluated empirically using the pointwise log-likelihood matrix \(LL \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times N}\).

The log-likelihood entry for observation \(i\) under draw \(s\) is:

\[LL_{i,s} = \log p\left(y_i \mid \beta^{(s)}, \sigma^{2(s)}\right) = -\frac{1}{2} \log\left(2\pi \sigma^{2(s)}\right) - \frac{\left(y_i - x_i^\top \beta^{(s)}\right)^2}{2 \sigma^{2(s)}}\]

Pointwise Predictive Density (\(lppd_{\text{sample}}\))

The sample-based estimate averages likelihoods across all MCMC draws prior to taking the logarithm:

\[lppd_{\text{sample}} = \sum_{i=1}^n \log \left( \frac{1}{N} \sum_{s=1}^N p\left(y_i \mid \beta^{(s)}, \sigma^{2(s)}\right) \right) = \sum_{i=1}^n \log \left( \frac{1}{N} \sum_{s=1}^N \exp\left(LL_{i,s}\right) \right)\]

Model Complexity Penalty (\(p_{\text{WAIC}}\))

The empirical effective number of parameters (\(p_{\text{WAIC}, 2}\)) calculates the sample variance of the log-likelihood across posterior draws for each observation \(i\):

\[p_{\text{WAIC}} = \sum_{i=1}^n V_{s=1}^N \left( LL_{i,s} \right)\]

where the operator \(V_{s=1}^N \left( LL_{i,s} \right)\) represents the sample variance for observation \(i\):

\[V_{s=1}^N \left( LL_{i,s} \right) = \frac{1}{N - 1} \sum_{s=1}^N \left( LL_{i,s} - \bar{LL}_i \right)^2\]

and \(\bar{LL}_i = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{s=1}^N LL_{i,s}\) is the sample mean log-likelihood for observation \(i\).

4. Numerical Stability: The Log-Sum-Exp Trick

Computing \(\log \left( \frac{1}{N} \sum_{s=1}^N \exp(LL_{i,s}) \right)\) directly in standard floating-point arithmetic easily causes numerical underflow (if \(LL_{i,s}\) values are highly negative, \(\exp(LL_{i,s}) \to 0\)) or overflow.

To ensure numerical stability, we apply the Log-Sum-Exp identity:

Let \(c_i = \max_{s \in \{1, \ldots, N\}} LL_{i,s}\).

\[\begin{aligned} \log \left( \frac{1}{N} \sum_{s=1}^N \exp(LL_{i,s}) \right) &= \log \left( \frac{1}{N} \exp(c_i) \sum_{s=1}^N \exp(LL_{i,s} - c_i) \right) \\ &= c_i - \log(N) + \log \left( \sum_{s=1}^N \exp\left(LL_{i,s} - c_i\right) \right) \end{aligned}\]

Because \(LL_{i,s} - c_i \le 0\), the exponential terms \(\exp(LL_{i,s} - c_i)\) are strictly bounded in \((0, 1]\), completely eliminating overflow and preventing underflow for all dominant posterior draws.

  N = N_draws

)

5. Sequential Model Comparison & Visualization

To observe how \(lppd\), \(p_{\text{WAIC}}\), and total \(\text{WAIC}\) evolve as parameters are added, we evaluate a sequence of nested Bayesian regression models:

  • \(M_0\): Intercept only (\(\beta_0\))
  • \(M_1\): Intercept + \(X_1\)
  • \(M_2\): Intercept + \(X_1 + X_2\)
  • \(\vdots\)
  • \(M_p\): Full model with all \(p\) predictors

Because \(p_{\text{WAIC}, \text{exact}}\) and \(lppd_{\text{exact}}\) give closed-form results instantly, we compute the exact metrics across all model sub-structures.

Methodological Note: How WAIC Estimates Out-of-Sample Performance

A fundamental challenge in statistical modeling is evaluating how well a model will generalize to new, unseen data (\(y^{\text{rep}}\)) drawn from the true data-generating process \(f(y)\).

Evaluating predictive accuracy directly on training data yields an overly optimistic assessment because the posterior distribution is optimized using those exact same observations. While cross-validation addresses this by physically partitioning the data (holding out subsets), WAIC estimates out-of-sample predictive accuracy analytically using within-sample pointwise posterior distributions:

  1. In-Sample Fit (\(lppd\)): Evaluates total predictive accuracy across observed data points, integrated over the posterior distribution \(p(\theta \mid y)\).
  2. Optimism Penalty (\(p_{\text{WAIC}}\)): Quantifies overoptimism by measuring the pointwise posterior variance of the log-likelihood for each observation. This acts as an automated, data-driven measure of effective model complexity.

By adjusting the in-sample predictive density for overoptimism, WAIC yields an asymptotically unbiased estimate of expected out-of-sample log predictive density (\(\text{elpd}_{\text{out}}\)) mapped onto the deviance scale:

\[\text{WAIC} = -2 \cdot \widehat{\text{elpd}}_{\text{out}} = -2 \left( lppd - p_{\text{WAIC}} \right)\]

The Sequential Trade-Off Mechanism

When evaluating nested models (\(M_0 \subset M_1 \subset \dots \subset M_p\)): * Model Fit (\(lppd\)): Strictly improves (or non-decreases) as predictors are added, as additional parameters allow the model to fit training observations more tightly. * Complexity Penalty (\(p_{\text{WAIC}}\)): Increases as parameters are added, reflecting increased parameter variance across higher dimensions.

The WAIC trajectory plots this dynamic trade-off across the sequence. Underfit models suffer from poor \(lppd\) fit, while overfit models incur large \(p_{\text{WAIC}}\) penalties. The minimum point on the WAIC curve identifies the model structure predicted to minimize out-of-sample prediction error.
